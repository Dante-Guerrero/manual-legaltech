Introduction Despite the inevitable inconveniences, legacy systems will continue to be integral parts of many organizations; however, the integration of legacy systems with modern automation tools is really hard to achieve. This study examines limitations and key enablers of automating such systems with Power Automate Desktop. It examines existing real world situations and aims to provide practical advice in making the digital transformation a success. 2. Literature Review 2.1 The Evolution of RPA The flood of Robotic Process Automation (RPA) into the world’s industries has heralded a new time in which businesses can leverage RPA to optimize and automate their processes. According to Madakam et al (2019) RPA is an independent innovation that combines the robustness and compactness of software with the specially created hard-ware and networking components; it also involves the logic of the artificial intelligence. The technology is applicable to many other domains like HR, finance, logistics and healthcare. However, one of the things that has validated the technology as critical to digital transformation is that its ability to bring immediate value to operational workflows. Nevertheless, RPA is not a stand-alone technology, but it is often embedded as a synergistic platform with other advanced systems including machine learning (ML), deep learning, as well as blockchain technologies. Together, these technologies together contribute to the phenomenon of hyperautomation as defined by George Et al (2023) or simply put: the next logical evolution of automation. Whereas RPA is just an element of hyperautomated processes, hyperautomation unites RPA with machine learning (including analytics) for end-to-end automation across functions. Hyperautomation results in companies regaling tangible benefits such as significant time savings, cost savings, as well as better customer service all indicators of RPA’s potential for a legacy operations revolution. These systems were not created with modern integration and service-oriented paradigms in mind and their legacy architecture has an exposure that is not API ready for direct integration. And here where Microsoft Power Automate Desktop stands out in this scenario. As written in their case study of Malaysia’s vaccination center, Kannan & Jian (2022) highlight Power Automate Desktop was essential in filling this gap by automating hard inputs of records in a manual system into this online system without using traditional integration. However, the most significant challenge when integrating with legacy systems based on RPA is this. Mishra et al. (2019) discuss technological issues involved in utilization of the technology in the business process outsourcing (BPO) domain. The main issues include compatibility problems with the existing applications, performance bottlenecks, and mistakes with the data structure all of which can hinder the efficacy of RPA. In addition, because legacy systems have not been standardized in the processes, the RPA bots usually need to be customized in great details, which significantly increases the development time and the overhead to maintain it. According to Kilpeläinen (2019), RPA is not helpful if data input is considered highly structured. The problem with the legacy systems is that these processes are not all hard coded as rules and sometimes do have a bit of a subjective aspect and therefore you therefore require additional layers of intelligence through AI or ML. Lacity et al. (2015) claim that potential adopters are confused about what RPA can and cannot do. Illustrating successful deployments involves case studies and empirical evaluations that are essential in demonstrating successful RPA tool deployments in organization and letting organizations take informed decisions on how to go about choosing the RPA tool, deploying and scaling it up. The organizations must focus on repetitive, having rules based, and structured data flows to maximize value. This is supported by Polkhovskiy (2022) whereby she outlines the importance of first finding suitable candidate processes for automation first before going through large scale development. Also, he emphasizes documenting current workflows in meticulous detail, defining high level business requirements, testing extensively to ensure the automation’s accuracy and reliability. With this in mind, the visual flow builder and AI Builder feature that comes packaged with Power Automate Desktop give a more approachable start for teams who are less experienced with coding. Apart from the process selection, the cost is also an important issue. Open-source tools are rather more limited in terms of features and support and will offer viable options only for smaller businesses in situations where technical expertise is available. Perhaps, however, the best way is to use commercial tools for mission critical processes and open-source tools for smaller processes (a balanced approach). Not only that, a culture of innovation in the workplace, and encouraging employees to take part in RPA endeavours will serve in great returns too. According to Davlatov (2023), when employees are given to opportunities to identify themselves and automate the minor tasks, productiveness and joy at work enhance. Recommendations Merge integrate the legacy systems with Power Automate Desktop (PAD), organizations must prioritise strategic and tactical recommendations that do equally technical, operational and human dimensions of digital transformation. A phased implementation strategy is the most important recommendation. Most organizations that attempt full automate processes without a phased rollout will fail, experience disruption of existing process workflow, or show poor performance metrics in all process functions. Organizations start out with pilot projects of high volume, rule-based processes that can be used to test PAD viability with low risk. As long as high performance and profitability can be demonstrated, wider deployment is more justifiable financially and operationally. Pilot projects are internal case studies, which provide useful data related to expected benefits, potential issues, time to value etc. It has a data driven nature and strengthens the confidence of the stakeholders and helps in change management. The early involvement of cross functional team such as IT, operations, compliance and end users is probably another important recommendation. While integrating legacy systems into PAD is not a pure technical effort, it is only possible if business processes and their regulatory frames are understood in depth, as well as the interaction points with PAD users. By involving diverse teams early, automated workflows are avoided from failing to participate in critical compliance steps as well as failing to derail user behavior patterns that influence operational efficiency. Additionally, organizations have to implement a governance framework to automate sustainability. In general, regardless of type, working with legacy systems is very important for governance; the data structures in legacy systems generally are outdated, undocumented or prone to integrity issues. Roles, responsibilities of bot development, testing, deployment, monitoring, maintenance should be defined with a governance framework. In addition, it should set up its clear protocols of version control, rollback, auditing and documentation. Structured bot lifecycle management is equally critical and in particular is likely to be needed for bots on legacy systems given the tendency for bots working on such systems to break from the slightest UI change, system downtime or worse: data inconsistencies. All the bots, with their dependencies and change history, are introduced with a central repository, helping with efficient maintenance and lack of splitting of work. Furthermore, the framework must contain metrics for a bot’s performance over time composed of execution success rates, error rates and response times. Continuous improvement and long-term life time of automation initiatives are ensured using these metrics. When it comes to the most vulnerable part of any organization’s IT infrastructure, legacy systems are usually the ones we are talking about. Since PAD automates these systems, the surface area that can be exposed via unattended automation is also increased, and bots could put sensitive information at risk if configured to access sensitive information or perform critical updates. The advice is to separate PAD bots on virtual machine (VM) with limited network access, no direct access to the internet, and strict role-based access controls. For example, enforce multiple authentication factors such as multi factor authentication, encryption of all credentials, auditing of bot activity logs frequently. Organizations working to regulatory practices like GDPR, HIPAA or SOX must make sure that their automation workflows are designed to stick to a data handling rule. It may entail the introduction of manual verification steps in some automated processes or keeping human in the loop decision points in workflows that involve personally identifiable information (PII). Governance and test teams should have a role in the design and testing of automation to head off violation of regulation. RPA development culture must be made to follow a compliance by design paradigm. Technically, organizations need to become invested in scalable infrastructure and their automation programs ought to be able to scale. One of the things is the absence of APIs in legacy systems. UI Automation that happens inside the PAD is inherently fragile and is hard to maintain. Wherever possible, such models of hybrid integration are recommended to explore models that capture both PAD and database/data based or API based integration. It would include working with IT to provide certain data through an API, or partnering with a third-party vendor to build a connector. Furthermore, using integration with Azure, SharePoint, Power BI, and Dynamics 365, it can significantly reduce the effort required to automate across end to end. Analytics and dashboard should be used to assess the impact PAD has over business KPIs. On the other hand, regular tracking of metrics including process completion time, error rate, cost saving, and the user satisfaction should be performed. Adding Power BI dashboards into the management review context gives leadership an opportunity to make data driven decisions on the expansion, modification or retirement of automation initiatives.
